The challenge

Good people spending time on repetitive coordination.

An operations concept exploring how structured intake, assisted routing, and human review could reduce repetitive coordination.

The hypothetical team moves information between inboxes, documents, spreadsheets, and internal tools. Duplicated effort is visible, but inconsistent intake and unclear ownership create the underlying friction.

The brief in four moves

  • 01Observe triggers, handoffs, and exceptions
  • 02Separate deterministic rules from judgement
  • 03Prototype one contained operational workflow
  • 04Define controls, fallbacks, and useful measures

The response

A connected flow with visible human control.

Strategy, content, interface design, and technology connect around one clear experience.

01 / INTAKE

Structure information at the start.

Relevant information arrives in one consistent format instead of being reconstructed from disconnected messages.

02 / ROUTE

Assist the repeatable decisions.

Rules and AI-supported classification suggest the next step while exceptions remain visible.

03 / REVIEW

Keep people accountable.

People approve sensitive or uncertain outputs before they move into downstream systems.

Experience direction

Workflow artefacts

Make the work visible
before automating it.

The concept maps the current friction, future flow, and human controls before defining implementation.

Current state · Fragmented

Work reconstructed at every step.

  1. Inbox request
  2. Manual data copy
  3. Unclear routing
  4. Status follow-up
Proposed state · Connected

One visible flow with review.

  1. Structured intake
  2. Assisted classification
  3. Human approval
  4. Tracked action
Automate

Formatting, classification suggestions, notifications, and status updates.

Review

Sensitive outputs, low-confidence classifications, and downstream actions.

Fallback

Missing data, exceptions, unavailable integrations, and manual ownership.
